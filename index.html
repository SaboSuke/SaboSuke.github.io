Business recovery plan

Make Wolvo self-sustaining.

Protect the active client, restore only the infrastructure the business can afford, invoice merchants directly for Wolvo's service, and acquire a small number of suitable merchants before investing in growth.

Priority: cash flowEarly Access: 1.000 DT / delivered orderConservative planning case: 80 DT / client / month90-day horizon
Decision rule: Do not spend personal money on restoring service until the provider confirms the amount, recovery terms, data status, and ongoing costs—and the company has a credible plan to retain the active client and collect revenue.

Client Lifecycle Process Flow

Key points:
  • Wolvo never handles client money directly - reduces risk and accounting complexity
  • Client receives full order value from Intigo, then pays Wolvo's service fee separately
  • Clear separation of concerns: Intigo handles delivery payment, Wolvo handles platform service
  • 7-day grace period ensures fair warning before any service disruption
  • All terms documented in signed Early Access agreement

1. Early Access pricing

Proposed starting offer: 1.000 DT for each delivered order. Keep the scope and billing rules clear, especially around returns, exchanges, cancellations, and disputes.

Commercial rule to document

  • Fee applies to each order meeting the agreed definition of “delivered”.
  • Define treatment of exchanges, duplicate orders, cancelled orders, and delivery reversals.
  • Specify monthly cut-off date, invoice date, payment due date, and dispute window.
  • Keep a line-level report so each invoice can be reconciled to order IDs and statuses.

Validate before launch

  • Confirm whether 1.000 DT is the final fee and how VAT, if applicable, is displayed.
  • Test the fee against typical merchant order volumes and competitors.
  • Make sure the price covers support, hosting, payment collection, and tax obligations.
  • Use a written Early Access agreement with a review date and clear termination terms.
At the current assumptions, 80 DT/client/month implies about 80 delivered orders per client each month. This is a hypothesis to verify with real client order data.

1. Payment and operating model

Current operating focus: Intigo pays merchants directly under its payout schedule; Wolvo invoices merchants separately for its service.

Step 1

Intigo → merchant

Intigo settles the merchant's order proceeds directly under its existing agreement and payout schedule.

Step 2

Wolvo measures usage

Generate a monthly delivered-order report with order IDs, statuses, billable quantities, unit fee, and exceptions.

Step 3

Wolvo → merchant invoice

Issue Wolvo's invoice, record payment, send reminders, and reconcile the invoice against the report.

Minimum billing controls

  • Use sequential invoice numbers and retain copies of invoices and supporting reports.
  • Set a due date and documented reminders.
  • Write down the seven-day grace period and any service-suspension procedure in the client agreement.
  • Give reasonable notice and define data export/access arrangements before suspension.
  • Record any founder cash advances or loans to the company with accountant-approved documentation.
Have the Tunisian accountant confirm the tax treatment and applicable electronic invoicing rules before issuing invoices under the new model. Do not assume the new process automatically resolves historical accounting.

2. Tunisia: legal and accounting checks

These are questions to verify with a Tunisian accountant or lawyer—not definitive legal advice.

Invoicing and tax

  • Which tax regime applies, and what filings or payments are overdue?
  • Does Wolvo need to issue electronic invoices for its software/services under the rules currently in force? Which enrollment and technical procedure applies?
  • What VAT treatment and invoice disclosures apply to the 1 DT per delivered-order fee?
  • What evidence is required to reconcile past money collected from delivery companies on behalf of merchants?
  • Does the stated 30 DT/month cover all tax-related obligations or only one specific payment?

Official reference: Ministry of Finance — invoicing obligations · Ministry of Finance — tax overview

Company bank account

  • Confirm bank requirements, authorized signatories, and current company documentation.
  • Separate company receipts and expenses from personal finances.
  • Ask the accountant how to document any personal funds used to keep the company operating.

Records to prepare

  • Company statutes and registration/tax documents.
  • Shareholder register and minutes of prior decisions.
  • Client and Intigo agreements.
  • Invoices, bank statements, tax declarations, and a list of unpaid liabilities.
Electronic invoicing rules and scope may have changed. Confirm the current rules directly with the accountant or the Ministry rather than relying on a general summary or a third-party article.

3. Shareholder deadlock

Do not assume every company action legally requires unanimous signatures just because that has been the practice so far. The answer depends on Wolvo's legal form, statutes, ownership percentages, and the type of decision.

Action list for a Tunisian corporate lawyer

  1. Review the company's legal form, statutes, and shareholder ownership.
  2. List the exact transactions or decisions being blocked and identify the legal basis for each signature requirement.
  3. Determine which ordinary operational actions the authorized manager can take without changing the statutes.
  4. Assess lawful options for negotiated share transfer, exit, mediation, or deadlock resolution.
  5. Confirm the voting thresholds and process required for fundraising or a capital increase.

Reference: Diwan — Tunisian Commercial Companies Code, French edition (2024). The applicable article depends on the company's form and proposed action; have counsel verify the current official text.

Do not bypass a legally required signature or attempt an informal removal. First establish exactly what the shareholder can legally block and what decisions can continue lawfully.
